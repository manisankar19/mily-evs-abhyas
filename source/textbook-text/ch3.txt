  4  Nature is home to a variety of plants,
  5  animals, birds and insects, each
  6  playing an important role in our world.
  7  Through observation and hands-on
  8  activities, students will explore the
  9  unique features of different plants
 10  and
 11  animals,
 12  understand
 13  their
 14  interdependence, and develop a deep
 15  appreciation for the environment.
 16  Through observation and experience,
 17  students will imbibe the spirit of
 18  ‘Vasudhaiva
 19  Kutumbakam’ — the
 20  world as one family.
 21  This unit highlights the connection
 22  between
 23  nature
 24  and
 25  culture,
 26  showing how traditional practices
 27  are associated with different aspects
 28  of nature. From studying plants and
 29  animals to discovering tribal art,
 30  and medicinal plants, students will
 31  engage in meaningful experiences that
 32  promote conservation and sustainable
 33  living. Interactive activities, including
 34  festivals and games, will help students
 35  recognise the harmony in nature and
 36  their role in protecting it.

  1  Note to the Teacher
  2  This unit consists of Chapter 3 — Nature Trail
  3  and Chapter 4 — Growing up with Nature. The
  4  key concepts covered in these chapters are
  5  listed below.
  6  Teacher to Facilitate
  7  Chapter 3
  8  •
  9  ‘Nature Trail’ guides students to explore
 10  the variety of plants and animals around
 11  them, and study their unique features.
 12  It shows how all animals and plants are
 13  deeply connected to each other. Students
 14  will observe the birds, leaves and animals,
 15  and play the ‘web of life’ game to understand
 16  the interdependence in nature and society.
 17  A variety of engaging activities and safety
 18  lessons help them appreciate, and care for
 19  the natural world.
 20  Chapter 4:
 21  •
 22  ‘Growing up with Nature’ portrays how
 23  nature and culture are deeply connected
 24  in our daily lives — from the food we eat
 25  to the traditions we follow. Students will
 26  discover some unique features of trees,
 27  flowers, tribal art and medicinal plants,
 28  while learning about the ways and means
 29  of sustainable living. They will also learn
 30  the medicinal values of different plants
 31  through some engaging activities and safety
 32  lessons that will help them appreciate, and
 33  protect their environment.
 34  •
 35  Teacher and students will
 36  collect various samples
 37  from nature, such as
 38  leaves, seeds, flowers, etc.,
 39  and may use in various
 40  activities.
 41  •
 42  Arrange nature walks,
 43  role-plays and ‘web of
 44  life’ games, and prepare
 45  observation sheets
 46  for students to record
 47  footprints, movements of
 48  birds and plant details.
 49  •
 50  Provide materials for leaf
 51  tracing, natural dye making
 52  and traditional house
 53  models, and guide students
 54  in food-picking activities
 55  and nature-inspired crafts.
 56  •
 57  Organise visits to nature
 58  parks, farms or plant
 59  nurseries, or invite experts
 60  from community or farmers
 61  to interact with students
 62  about nature conservation
 63  and traditional practices.

=== p.22 ===
  1  How to play?
  2  1. Each player gets a card with the name of a plant,
  3  animal, or natural element.
  4  2. One player starts by holding a ball of yarn and think
  5  about their connection to other player with similar
  6  cards.
  7  3. For example, a tree provides food for a bird or a river
  8  supports a fish, the yarn is passed to that player
  9  while the first player still holds onto its thread.
 10  4. The web keeps growing as more connections are
 11  made.
 12  5. Now, what if one part of the web disappears? Try
 13  removing a player, like a missing deer for a tiger or
 14  plants for animals.
 15  6. Watch how the web weakens or collapses. It shows
 16  how everything in nature is connected!
